The R.O.A.D. Framework

"Requirements force us to name what is at stake. Operationalizing data forces us to confront bias not as a nuisance variable but as a mirror of our society. Development makes fairness, drift, and calibration inseparable from accuracy. Deployment reminds us that intelligence without humility becomes surveillance."

Surgical Autonomy Paradox

"Machines can learn gestures; they can suture with remarkable precision. But they cannot integrate the lived complexity of a patient. They cannot weigh how comorbidities, fatigue, or subtle physiological cues change decisions. They cannot hold the ethical trade-offs of risk and benefit."
— The Operating Room Frontier

Level 1: da Vinci

Complete human control. Steadying the surgeon's hand.

Level 2: GRACE

Camera optimization. 20% reduction in procedure time.

Level 3: STAR

Outperformed surgeons in suturing. The accountability question emerges.

Ethics of Ephemerality

Intergenerational Ethics

Transparency as Compassion

Models that show limits respect humans.

The $40 Problem

"If this were a new drug, one case would not move policy. The same standard should apply to AI. A paid add-on turns risk prediction into a benefit for people who can afford it. If the tool is good enough to help, it belongs in the base pathway. If it is not ready for the base pathway, it is not ready to sell."
— Clinical Medicine Ethics

What NOT to Do

Never: Deploy black-box models without calibrated probabilities
Never: Scale algorithms trained on single-site retrospective data
Never: Trust LLM claims without primary source verification
Never: Allow autonomy for irreversible surgical decisions
Never: Rely on accuracy alone without considering recall/precision trade-offs
